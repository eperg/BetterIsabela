Brief History of Isabela

25,000+ years ago

Isabela's earliest known inhabitants included the Agta peoples, followed over time by the ancestors of today's Ibanag, Gaddang, Yogad, and other communities.

16th–18th centuries

Spanish rule introduced Christianity, the encomienda system, and the tobacco monopoly; local chieftains Dabo and Marayag led notable revolts.

1856

A royal decree created Isabela de Luzon from parts of Cagayan and Nueva Vizcaya, named it for Queen Isabella II, and established Ilagan as its capital.

1901

American forces captured President Emilio Aguinaldo in Palanan on March 23, making the province a pivotal setting in the Philippine-American War.

Late 19th–20th centuries

Large-scale Ilocano migration joined Isabela's many ethnolinguistic groups, strengthening its identity as the Melting Pot of the North.

1942–1945

Isabela endured Japanese occupation during World War II and was liberated with the rest of the country in 1945.

Today

The province comprises 3 cities, 34 municipalities, and 1,055 barangays, with agriculture remaining central to its economy and identity.

Queen Province of the North

Named for Queen Isabella II of Spain, Isabela is widely known by this enduring provincial title.

Agricultural Heartland

Its cities and municipalities produce rice, corn, and other high-value crops celebrated through the annual Bambanti Festival.
